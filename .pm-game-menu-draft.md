# Party-game menu — family/group around a TV, each with a phone

**Workstream:** GeoParty framework extraction · **Date:** 2026-08-23 · **Status:** DRAFT for review
**Constraint:** static no-build GitHub Pages, Firebase RTDB, no server code (same as GeoParty). Couch + everyone-plays modes.

Each idea is scored on two axes:
- **Fun for a family around a TV** (the product bar)
- **Framework stress** (what new sync/concurrency problem it forces the kernel to solve — the whole point of building 2-3 proof games)

---

## Tier 1 — highest framework stress (best extraction signal)

### 🏁 1. Flag Reveal (the idea you already had)
A flag reveals piece-by-piece (tiles + blur→sharp). Players ring in; first correct wins.
- **Framework stress:** HIGH — first-wins **buzzer arbitration** (a genuinely new contention class GeoParty never had) + **private per-phone state** (wrong ring hidden). The single most instructive build.
- **Fun:** strong for a family — fast, visual, everyone physically leans in.
- **Dataset:** ✅ solved (flagcdn.com + `mledoze/countries`, both open).

### 🎭 2. The Impostor / "One of Us Is Lying" (social deduction)
TV shows a category (e.g. "fruits"). Everyone but one player sees a real answer (e.g. "banana"); the impostor sees nothing and must fake it. Players vote who's faking, or impostor survives.
- **Framework stress:** **private per-phone state** (each phone sees different info — TV can't show it). GeoParty has *zero* hidden info; this forces the "hidden subtree" contract hard.
- **Fun:** family classic (think Spyfall/Among Us on a TV). High replayability.
- **Dataset:** needs a word/category bank — easy to bundle.

### ✍️ 3. Quiplash-style / "Fibbage" family prompt games (write-your-answer)
TV shows a prompt ("Name a worse song to play at a wedding"). Everyone writes a funny answer; one is the "real" answer (for Fibbage-style) or everyone votes for the funniest (Quiplash).
- **Framework stress:** **each phone submits independently + a voting/arbitration phase** — new submission + tabulation pattern, but *not* a first-wins race. Moderate stress.
- **Fun:** **the single most proven family party mechanic** — Jackbox's crown jewel.
- **Dataset:** prompt bank (curate). **No images needed.**

### 🎤 4. "Never Have I Ever" / "Would You Rather" / "Most Likely To" (family-safe)
TV flashes a prompt; all players answer privately on phones, TV reveals the tally + who picked what. "Most likely to get lost in a mall" → everyone secretly points at one player.
- **Framework stress:** **simultaneous private votes + real-time reveal tally.** The tally must be computed without contention — another good arbitration test.
- **Fun:** excellent with a real family group; zero knowledge needed to play.

### 5. Family Feud style (survey match)
TV shows a survey question ("Things you'd find in Grandma's purse"). Everyone guesses the top answers; matching the #1 rank = points; "X" strikes.
- **Framework stress:** moderate — multiple-choice-ish + a strike/turn machine. Similar to GeoParty's existing phase machine, so **lower** stress.
- **Fun:** proven (Family Feud is a living-room staple). Needs a survey-answer dataset (the hard part to source).

---

## Tier 2 — lighter stress, easy wins

### 6. Pictionary / Drawful (draw on phone, TV shows it)
Players draw a prompt on their phone; others guess the word by typing. TV shows the canvases.
- **Framework stress:** **async drawing sync** (canvas data) — genuinely new (GeoParty never streams drawing input). Moderate.
- **Fun:** proven.

### 7. 20 Questions / Akinator-style
TV silently picks something; players ask yes/no questions, TV narrows it down.
- **Framework stress:** low (single player vs TV — not multi-phone contention). Not a great extraction signal.
- **Fun:** okay solo-ish; weak as a *party* mechanic.

### 8. Charades / taboo
TV shows a word to the actor; the actor performs it; others shout guesses (no phones needed for guessing).
- **Framework stress:** near-zero (phones barely used). Skip as a proof game.

---

## What I'd recommend (PM position)

**Build two proof games, chosen for complementary stress — NOT three similar ones.**

- **Game A — Flagged (buzzer + private state).** This is your strongest first build. It hits two of the kernel's three known gaps at once and stays tiny (2D static, no map imagery layer). The one risk to design around: **make the buzzer the headline**, not a garnish — tune reveal timing so rings genuinely collide, or the experiment learns nothing.
- **Game B — Impostor/One-of-Us (pure private information).** Complements A: where Flagged stresses *first-wins contention*, Impostor stresses *hidden per-phone state*. Together they'd pin down the two biggest open questions about the kernel.
- **Defer Quiplash/Fibbage-style** to a phase-2 — its submission+tabulation is interesting but lower-stress than the two above; it's also the most content-hungry (needs a curated word bank).

**Why two and not three:** the sunk-cost risk. Game 2 establishes the seam; game 3 is where momentum takes over from evidence. If both proof games hold clean, we have enough to write the kernel spec.

## Honest risks
- **Buzzer fairness is the make-or-break.** If a wrong ring is public-and-punished it's louder; if private it preserves suspense. Design decision, not solved.
- **Not every game idea is worth it** — the ones that stress the framework least (20 Questions, charades) are the fastest to build but teach the kernel nothing.
- **Same-shape trap:** avoid picking three geography/puzzle games. The menu above is deliberately cross-mechanic.
